Reality check

The competitive landscape, corrected

v2 positioned "no bank login, ever" as close to unique in the market. It's a real and defensible stance — but it's not undiscovered territory. Here's who's actually already there.

CP
CardPointers — the one that matters most

Manual card entry, no bank connection, reward-category tracking and recommendations. Free tier (1 card). CardPointers+ is $7.50/mo or $90/yr, often discounted; a $196 lifetime option also exists. Reviewed across a dozen finance sites in 2026 — this is an established, still-operating product, not an abandoned one.

Same core model as KovaSubscription-primaryYears in market
MR
MaxRewards — the connected alternative

MaxRewards Gold is $108/yr, but the features that actually differentiate it — account sync, auto-activation of rotating categories — require Plaid bank linking. This is the segment Kova's "no bank login, ever" stance is explicitly built to not compete with, and shouldn't try to.

Requires bank linkingHigher price point

What this actually means: the "zero competitors doing this without a bank login" claim was wrong. The corrected claim is narrower and still true: Kova is a credit-based, no-login alternative to CardPointers — same category, different bet on data model and (per Step 10 of the build) freshness/verification rigor as the differentiator, not privacy alone. That's a real, ownable wedge. It's a harder pitch than "nobody else does this," but it's an honest one, and CardPointers surviving for years on subscription revenue for this exact feature set is direct evidence people pay for it.

Revenue model

What changed from v2 to v3 — and why

v1 → v2 flipped from subscription-primary to affiliate-primary because affiliate has zero marginal cost and a great trigger moment. That reasoning was sound in theory. v3 flips back toward subscription for a different, evidence-based reason: one real affiliate application has now actually been rejected, and research confirms this is the normal first outcome for small, pre-launch apps — not a fluke. Affiliate isn't gone. It's demoted from "primary engine" to "upside layer," because it depends on approvals you don't control yet.

v2 modeled
Affiliate CPA as the primary engine, funding the business from Month 5–9. Subscription as the secondary engine.
v3 uses
Subscription (Kova Pro, annual + a lifetime option) as the near-term primary engine — provably works (CardPointers), doesn't require anyone's approval. Affiliate stays as upside that compounds once multiple network relationships land.
v2 modeled
$49.99/yr, single subscription tier, no one-time option.
v3 uses
Keep $49.99/yr as the value-priced entry tier — cheaper than both CardPointers ($90) and MaxRewards ($108) — but add a one-time lifetime purchase (~$129–149) for subscription-averse, privacy-conscious users who'd otherwise never convert. CardPointers' own $196 lifetime tier proves this segment exists and pays.
v2 modeled
Affiliate access via a single network relationship (Impact, one attempt).
v3 uses
Apply to CardRatings, Fintel Connect, and CommissionSoup in addition to the FlexOffers/Bankrate/Impact attempts already made. CardRatings in particular exists specifically for smaller finance publishers who don't qualify for direct issuer relationships yet.
v2 modeled
"Creator rev-share" as a channel — real but undefined, no pricing, no first-10 plan.
v3 uses
A costed nano/micro creator sponsorship plan (see Go-to-market below) with real 2026 rate benchmarks, so this is a budget line, not an aspiration.

Affiliate — the real state

One rejection isn't a verdict. Here's the fuller path.

What's already in motion
  • FlexOffers — submitted, pending review
  • Bankrate / RVMediaNetwork — submitted, pending review
  • Impact.com — blocked until a public App Store URL exists (their mobile-app channel requires apps.apple.com, not a TestFlight link)
Three more worth applying to
  • CardRatings — built specifically for smaller finance publishers who can't get direct issuer relationships; single partnership gives access to a broad range of card offers
  • Fintel Connect — dedicated financial-services affiliate network, 5,000+ vetted publisher network including apps and podcasters, not just blogs
  • CommissionSoup — finance-focused network for publishers with an audience interested in personal finance

The honest planning assumption for v3: expect approval timelines measured in months, not weeks, and expect more rejections before an acceptance — that's the normal shape of this process for a pre-launch app, per the same research that flagged CardRatings as the network built for exactly this situation. Don't gate any other roadmap decision on affiliate approval landing on any particular date.

Go-to-market

A costed creator plan, not a hope

"Creator rev-share" in v2 was directionally right but had no numbers attached. Here's what it actually costs to run, using real 2026 rate data — finance-niche creators charge 2–3x normal rates because their audiences convert at genuinely higher intent, but the nano tier is still cheap enough to bootstrap.

TierFollowersTypical cost / postWhy it fits Kova
Nano1K–10K$25–250Cheapest entry point; credit-card-niche nano creators often already post organically about rewards optimization
Micro10K–100K$200–2,000Best ROI tier once early traction/testimonials exist to show them
Official influencer programs100K–2.5M+—Follower minimums this high are out of reach pre-revenue; skip for now, revisit post-Series-A-or-not
The first-10 outreach plan
  • Identify 20–30 nano/micro creators already posting organic credit-card-rewards content on TikTok and Instagram Reels (search "credit card hacks," "rewards optimization," "no bank login")
  • Offer flat-fee sponsorship (not pure rev-share) for the first 10 — rev-share alone under-motivates creators with no track record of your conversion rate yet
  • Give each a unique promo mention ("use code [creator] for early access") tracked via a simple referral param, not a full attribution platform — no need to over-build this before it's proven
  • Budget: 10 creators × $150 avg (nano-tier) = ~$1,500 to test the channel before scaling to micro-tier spend
Where the audience already is
  • Personal-finance and credit-card subreddits — organic presence (answering questions, not promotional posts) builds credibility before any paid push
  • TikTok and Instagram Reels over YouTube long-form — finance/investing content on TikTok specifically commands a CPV premium (3–5x entertainment content), signaling real audience purchase intent, not just views
  • Gen Z awareness of card products skews toward Instagram/TikTok discovery over traditional channels — aligns with where Kova's actual target user (multi-card holder, rewards-motivated) already spends attention

Decision

Is this still worth filing an LLC for?

The case for yes
  • A real competitor (CardPointers) has sustained subscription revenue on this exact model for years — that's market validation, not a warning sign
  • Kova's actual differentiation narrows to "verification-rigor + freshness" (your weekly Scout/Auditor process is a real, hard-to-copy moat) rather than "privacy" alone — still a legitimate wedge
  • Subscription no longer depends on any external approval — you control that timeline entirely
  • One rejection is normal, expected, and doesn't close the affiliate door; three more networks remain untried
What to watch honestly
  • You're now entering a market with an established, well-reviewed incumbent — differentiation on data quality has to be real and visible in the product, not just claimed in marketing copy
  • v3's Year 3 base case ($620K) is intentionally lower than v2's ($750K) — this is the honest number, not a sales pitch to yourself
  • Gate 2 from v2 still applies and is worth keeping: if subscription alone can't hit meaningful install→paid conversion by Month 9, that's the real signal to reassess, not any single affiliate rejection

Bottom line: the business model survives the correction. What doesn't survive is the assumption that affiliate revenue was ever going to be fast or guaranteed — it wasn't, and now there's real evidence instead of a hopeful projection. Subscription-first, with a lifetime tier and a costed (not aspirational) creator channel, is a plan you can execute without waiting on anyone's approval. That's a fundable, buildable business — smaller than v2 promised, but real.
